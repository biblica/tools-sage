# Protected rewrite-detail rules

These rules are non-negotiable for every BIC REWRITE and SELF-CHECK task. Workflow simplification, prompt compression, Skill adaptation, or ACT generation must not weaken or abbreviate them.

## Complete source authority

The routed contemporary source USFM is the complete and exclusive content authority. Every bounded source content unit must have a target-language representation. No semantic content may be added or omitted.

Preserve, contextually and functionally:

- every proposition, participant, referent, action, state, relation, possession, identification, negation, modality, number, comparison, condition, purpose, cause, result, and speech function;
- time reference, tense contribution, aspect, mood, voice, agency, responsibility, information focus, event structure, and participant continuity;
- honorifics, titles, kinship terms, social relationships, politeness, status, and interpersonal force;
- connectors, clause relationships, information sequence, topic-focus structure, ambiguity, repetition, parallelism, contrast, emphasis, genre, literary form, quotation function, and rhetorical force;
- source-significant capitalisation, including divine-referent distinctions when established by source and context;
- every human-readable USFM payload and every source marker, level, caller, reference target, attribute, paired marker, nested `\+` marker identity, heading, poetry line, footnote, cross-reference, `\add`, `\nd`, `\qt`, `\wj`, and other marked content.

## Prohibited transformations

Do not add, omit, infer, explain, harmonise, generalise, specify, intensify, soften, compress, expand, modernise, doctrinally clarify, resolve genuine ambiguity, or substitute familiar Bible wording.

Do not erase repetition, merge distinct source constructions, flatten discourse relationships, hide agency, change responsibility, normalise honorifics, or remove source-supported ambiguity.

## Minimum-departure rule

Use target-language grammar and function words only where required to express the same source content. Use the smallest sufficient restructuring. When two renderings are equally accurate and natural, choose the rendering closer to source structure and information order.

## Capitalisation

Preserve source-significant capitalisation contextually. Divine `Dia`, `Ia`, or `-Nya` may require capitalised target equivalents when local source evidence establishes a divine referent. Ordinary sentence-initial capitalisation is not proof of divinity. When referent identity is genuinely ambiguous, preserve the ambiguity as far as the target language permits and record `CAPITALIZATION_OR_REFERENT_AMBIGUITY` rather than inventing a referent.

## Index and memory authority

Apply an Active memory or index entry only to its linked sense and only when it agrees with local source evidence. Review state is not a usage gate. Approved Operator decisions are protected from silent overwrite. Global frequency never overrides local source evidence.

For the current SAGE memory model, `APPROVED_FOR_USE` is the usable Active state. Human review receipts record attention and provenance but do not authorise or block stage progression.

## USFM integrity

Retain the source content-marker sequence inside scope unless a documented USFM-standard exception is required. Rewrite human-readable payloads into the target language, but never delete, empty, flatten, unpair, reorder, or invent markers. Preserve machine metadata unless the task explicitly manages it. `START` content is all target-language prechapter content after `\id`, `\usfm`, and `\ide` and before the first `\c`.

The generated marker policy may permit normalisation of layout-only paragraph or poetry marker levels. That exception does not authorise any change to semantic marker identity, distinct heading or poetry-line content units, payload order, paired-marker integrity, nested `\+` identity, callers, reference targets, attributes, notes, cross-references, coordinate coverage, or protected marked content.
